CvarcLogger is a new logging program, built from the ground up in-house rather than bought off the shelf — but "new" doesn't mean narrow, and it no longer means Windows-only, either. The same program that logs a routine ragchew on 20 meters also tracks a SOTA summit activation or a POTA park activation automatically, with no separate spreadsheet or website tab required. Version 1.37 adds a real Windows installer and a round of Settings-screen polish; alongside it, the club's Android companion app, CvarcCellLog, has quietly cleared its own first public version — the same log, the same lookups, now in a pocket instead of just on a desk.

CvarcLogger was written by CVARC Technical Director Charles S. Maslin, W6CSM, who has been running his own day-to-day logging through it since early development. Station profiles, DXCC lookups, and the rest of CvarcLogger's feature set are designed around individual operation, and can be switched at any time to reflect operation under the club's shared callsign, AA6CV. Log a contact and the software does the busywork a paper logbook never could: it resolves the DXCC entity, CQ zone, and ITU zone straight from the callsign and grid square, keeps UTC and local time in sync for late or backdated entries, and — when a radio is connected over Hamlib — pulls frequency, band, and mode straight off the rig as you tune.

What CvarcLogger Does

At its core, CvarcLogger is built for fast, in-the-chair logging: type a callsign, click Lookup (or just log the contact — the lookup runs automatically either way), and the operator's name, grid square, city, state, county, and country fill in from Callook.info, QRZ.com, or QRZCQ.com, whichever the station has configured. ARRL Section is derived automatically from state and county rather than looked up separately, and CQ/ITU zone resolve from the grid square itself, which matters for split-zone countries like the United States. Every field on the entry form — RST, name, grid, location, ARRL Section, zones, SOTA/POTA references, operator, QTH, transmit power, and a free-text comment — can be shown or hidden to match how an operator actually works, and the choice is remembered between sessions.

  • ZonesDXCC, CQ, and ITU zones auto-resolve from callsign and grid square — no lookup tables to consult by hand.
  • Rig linkLive CAT control over Hamlib keeps frequency, band, and mode following the radio automatically.
  • Digital modesWSJT-X can hand logged QSOs straight to CvarcLogger over UDP, no manual ADIF import required.
  • Live mapOptional GridTracker2 broadcast puts every new contact on the map the moment it's logged.
  • RecordsFull ADIF import/export keeps the log portable and compatible with everything else in the shack.

Now Tracking Four Awards, Not Two

Awards Progress — one window, opened from the View menu — has quietly become the most feature-dense corner of the program. DXCC and Worked All States were there from early on, each entity or state checked off automatically as contacts land in the log, worked and confirmed counted separately. A recent update added two more tabs built around the same idea: no separate spreadsheet, no manual tally, just whatever is already in the log.

The new Mountain Goat (SOTA) tab tracks progress toward SOTA's 1,000-point activator award. Add a summit code and its point value and official name are looked up automatically; log a contact with the summit entered in the entry form's My SOTA field and the tracker picks it up on its own. SOTA's real rule — that an activation only counts once at least four contacts are logged from the summit on the same UTC date — is enforced automatically, with an unqualified summit's points shown in parentheses, like (4), so an operator can see at a glance what a summit would be worth once the fourth contact comes in.

The new Parks on the Air (POTA) tab works the same way for park activations, tracking progress toward POTA's tiered activator awards — Bronze at 10 parks, climbing through Silver, Gold, Platinum, Diamond, Ruby, and Emerald at 125 — plus the separate Kilo award for 1,000 cumulative contacts logged at a single park. A park counts as activated once ten different stations have been worked from it on the same UTC date. Park names resolve live from POTA's own public API where a connection is available, falling back to a bundled offline park directory of more than 93,000 locations worldwide when it isn't.

Calling all activators CvarcLogger's SOTA and POTA tracking is brand new, and the club is looking for a few more members who actually get out and activate — summits, parks, or both — to put it through its paces before a wider release. If that's you, the Technical Desk wants to hear from you.

When Two Programs Want the Same Port

Version 1.34's fix started with a familiar troubleshooting moment: WSJT-X was logging contacts, CvarcLogger's WSJT-X receiver was enabled, and nothing was showing up. No error, no warning — just silence. The cause turned out to be a real-world collision rather than a bug in the usual sense: GridTracker2 was already listening on the same network port WSJT-X broadcasts to, and Windows will only ever deliver a given UDP packet to one of two programs sharing a port, never both. CvarcLogger's receiver was bound, healthy, and simply never handed a single packet.

The fix leans on a feature GridTracker2 already had: its own "Forward UDP messages" relay. WSJT-X still broadcasts to GridTracker2 exactly as before; GridTracker2 logs the contact for its own map, then forwards the same message on to CvarcLogger on a separate port. CvarcLogger's own outbound broadcast to GridTracker2 (for QSOs typed in directly) got a dedicated port too, matching GridTracker2's "Receive ADIF UDP (Broadcasts for Logging)" setting. Both directions are now confirmed working with real WSJT-X traffic, not just a hand-built test packet.

An Actual Installer, and a Tidier First Run

Three point releases since 1.34 have been quieter but no less real. WSJT-X-relayed contacts now get the same automatic online lookup as anything typed in by hand, filling Name, Grid, Country, State, County, City, and ARRL Section without overwriting whatever the over-the-air exchange already supplied. CAT-connected radios now report transmit power alongside frequency, band, and mode, converted from the raw fraction Hamlib reports into real watts using a per-radio "Max Power" setting. Log row numbers now count chronologically — the oldest QSO is always row 1 — regardless of how the grid is currently sorted or searched.

Version 1.37 turns its attention to the parts of the program a new member sees first. CvarcLogger now installs like any other Windows program: a proper CvarcLogger-Setup-1.37.exe puts it in the Windows "Apps & Features" list with a real uninstall entry, alongside the existing no-install portable zip for anyone who'd rather just run it from a folder. Settings got a pass for clarity — QRZ.com and QRZCQ.com credential status now reads "Configured" or "Not Configured" instead of a bare True/False, each password field has a show/hide toggle, and every Radio Control slot's Hamlib dropdown gained a "-none-" entry to explicitly clear a slot instead of leaving a stale model ID behind. Station Profiles now marks its two required fields, Callsign and UTC Offset, with an asterisk. And a fresh install now starts with CAT, automatic rigctld launch, GridTracker2 broadcast, and WSJT-X log receiving all switched off by default, rather than quietly assuming a new user has already wired up a shack full of other software.

CvarcCellLog Ships Its First Release

The Android companion app mentioned as "early builds" just a couple of releases ago is no longer early. CvarcCellLog reached its Milestone 1 goal in full — boot, database migration, and logging, viewing, searching, editing, and deleting a QSO, all built on the exact same Core and Data code CvarcLogger runs on the desktop — and then kept going. Version 1.02 is out now as a signed APK, with its own user manual, and a changelog of its own to match: a Show/Hide toggle on the QRZ.com and QRZCQ.com password fields, a Lookup button added to the Edit QSO screen (previously only on the entry form), a fix for four fields (UTC Offset, Frequency, CQ Zone, ITU Zone) that were silently suppressing Android's on-screen keyboard, and a redesigned QSO Log — one line per record, a header row that stays put while the list scrolls, and a new Columns screen to choose which fields show and in what order. As of 1.02, tapping a column header sorts the log by it, with a second tap reversing the direction, mirroring how a desktop spreadsheet behaves.

Stress-tested, not just tapped a few times Before shipping, CvarcCellLog's log screen was pointed at a real 5,676-QSO ADIF export and confirmed to import, scroll, and search without choking — not just a handful of test rows. Verification so far has all been on an Android emulator; testing on a real phone (a Samsung Galaxy S24) is the next item on the list before a wider release.

What's Still Ahead for the Log in Your Pocket

CAT control, GridTracker broadcast, and full ADIF import/export polish are the next milestones on CvarcCellLog's list — QRZ.com/QRZCQ.com lookup, editable Station Profiles, and a Settings screen already made it in ahead of schedule. The Android app shares CvarcLogger's Core and Data code outright, so a contact logged in one program is a contact the other can see the moment the two are pointed at the same database file — the field-to-desk workflow the whole effort was started for.

CvarcLogger 1.37 is available both ways: run CvarcLogger-Setup-1.37.exe for a normal Windows install, or grab the portable zip and run it from anywhere with no install step at all. CvarcCellLog 1.02 ships as a signed APK with its manual alongside it. Both changelogs, as always, ship in the box.
